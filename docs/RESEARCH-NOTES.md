## ⚠️ The single most important fact on this page

**The Brownsville Ship Channel was deepened in June 2026. The old 42 ft figure is wrong.**

| Reach | Before | **Now (June 2026)** |
|---|---|---|
| Main channel | 42 ft | **52 ft** |
| Entrance & jetty channels (Brazos Santiago Pass) | 44 ft | **54 ft** |

The Brazos Island Harbor (BIH) Channel Improvement Project completed and was ribbon-cut **June 25, 2026**, a ~$539M USACE Galveston District / Port of Brownsville / NextDecade public-private partnership — one of four national P3 pilots and the only navigation project among them. This makes Brownsville the **third deepest port in Texas**.

> The buildout plan (§7) uses "the Brownsville Ship Channel's 42-foot project depth" as its example of grounded copy. **That example is now out of date.** Any page that says 42 ft is publishing a stale number about the one thing this business is supposed to be expert in. Use 52 ft.

Sources: [USACE / DVIDS ribbon-cutting](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/printable/568390) · [Port of Brownsville](https://www.portofbrownsville.com/port-of-brownsville-celebrates-completion-of-brazos-island-harbor-channel-improvement-project/) · [RGV Business Journal](https://www.rgvbusinessjournal.com/news/25/06/2026/port-of-brownsvilles-539m-project-creates-one-of-the-gulfs-deepest-channels-allowing-larger-vessels-to-call/)

### Ship recycling — the defining local industry

- The port captures **more than 85% of US Navy and MARAD ship-recycling business**.
- **SteelCoast** (Hilco Redevelopment Partners + MCM Marine Services JV, formed May 2017 out of the former ESCO Marine) operates an **80-acre complex with roughly 4,000 ft of water frontage on the main channel**.

Sources: [Port of Brownsville — America's Premier Ship Recycling Port](https://www.portofbrownsville.com/americas-premier-ship-recycling-port/) · [PRNewswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/steelcoast-a-joint-venture-of-hilco-redevelopment-partners-and-mcm-marine-services-completes-purchase-to-save-70-acre-ship-recycling-and-scrap-processing-complex-in-brownsville-texas-300449702.html) · [Marine Log](https://www.marinelog.com/news/brownsville-ship-recycling-yard-has-new-owners-and-new-name/)

**Why this matters to a diving contractor:** layberth and tow-in work, pre-tow condition surveys, hull and cofferdam work on vessels awaiting scrapping, and dropped-object recovery around cutting berths. This is a genuine local specialism that Houston-based competitors cannot claim.

### LNG

- **Rio Grande LNG** (NextDecade) — 48 MTPA at full build, ~1,000-acre site, FID reached on 5 of 10 potential trains. Phase 1 (~$400M) includes channel deepening at the site plus **two ship berths and a turning basin**.
- A **second LNG export terminal, Texas LNG**, is also progressing at the port (Feb 2026).
- Rio Grande LNG filed for six new support buildings (~$6M) in July 2026.

Sources: [NextDecade](https://www.next-decade.com/our-business/rio-grande-lng/) · [RGV Business Journal](https://www.rgvbusinessjournal.com/news/29/07/2026/rio-grande-lng-6-million-support-buildings-port-of-brownsville/) · [MyRGV](https://myrgv.com/publications/the-monitor/2026/02/12/texas-lng-project-moves-forward-at-port-of-brownsville/)

## Brownsville–Port Isabel shrimp fleet

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Current fleet | ~140 boats |
| National rank | 2nd by value and volume of wild-caught shrimp |
| State rank | Largest and most valuable commercial fishery in Texas |
| Historic peak | ~500 boats, mid-1980s |
| Pressures | Deckhand shortage, diesel cost, farm-raised imports |

Sources: [MyRGV](https://myrgv.com/publications/the-brownsville-herald/2024/07/12/brownsville-shrimping-industry-struggles-against-farm-raised-imports/) · [Port of Brownsville](https://www.portofbrownsville.com/gulf-shrimp-season-under-way/)

**Why this matters:** a 140-boat trawler fleet is a recurring source of wrapped propellers, net and cable fouling, and running-gear damage — high-frequency, fast-converting work distinct from the port's deep-draft commercial trade.

## Regulatory — cite these exactly

### OSHA 29 CFR 1910 Subpart T (Commercial Diving Operations)

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Minimum team, surface-supplied air | **3** — designated person-in-charge, diver, and a tender; the diver must be **continuously tended** while in the water |
| Depth limit, surface-supplied air | **190 fsw**; dives with bottom times ≤30 min may go to **220 fsw** |
| Decompression chamber | Must be **ready at the dive location** for any dive outside no-decompression limits or **deeper than 100 fsw** |
| Bell | Required where in-water decompression exceeds **120 minutes** (exceptions: heavy gear, physically confining spaces) |

Key sections: §1910.410 (dive team qualifications), §1910.425 (surface-supplied air diving).

Sources: [eCFR Subpart T](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/subtitle-B/chapter-XVII/part-1910/subpart-T) · [OSHA §1910.425](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.425) · [OSHA CPL 02-00-151](https://www.osha.gov/enforcement/directives/cpl-02-00-151)

### ABS UWILD / in-water survey

- Examination of the **entire vessel below the waterline**, by an **ABS-approved diver**, using **closed-circuit television with two-way communication**.
- The diver must be employed by a **firm approved by the Bureau as a service supplier** — this is the credential gate. See **B3**.
- Dive progress **monitored by the attending Surveyor**, and/or photographically documented, depending on vessel age and type.
- **Water clarity must be sufficient to view the full height of the propeller and rudder in a single frame**; visibility typically needs to exceed ~2 m.
- Best conducted in a **protected location with calm, clear water**.

Sources: [ABS UWILD Guide (PDF)](https://ww2.eagle.org/content/dam/eagle/rules-and-guides/archives/generic/supplementalguidesforoptlclassnot/uwild_guide.pdf) · [DNV bottom survey in-water](https://www.dnv.com/services/bottom-survey-in-water/) · [USCG NVIC 01-89 Ch.1 underwater survey guidance](https://www.thetvib.org/2025/07/22/uscg-revisions-to-nvic-01-89-change-1-underwater-survey-guidance-uwild-considerations/)

> **The commercial point.** The ABS visibility criterion is the honest reason a Brownsville UWILD is scheduled rather than improvised: the channel is working water, and clarity is the variable that decides whether a survey is acceptable to the attending Surveyor. A contractor who explains this ranks for it — and is the one the surveyor calls back.
